Where it breaks

Most RevOps problems are not tooling problems. They are agreement problems.

Two teams, two definitions, one report that nobody trusts. No amount of new software fixes that, which is why the last three tools you bought did not.

  • Marketing counts a lead at the form fill. Sales counts it at the first reply. Both numbers are on the same slide.
  • The pipeline has nine stages, four of which mean "we are waiting to hear back."
  • Lifecycle stage moves backwards, forwards and sideways, and no report survives it.
  • Attribution is settled by whoever presents last.
  • The handoff between sales and onboarding happens in a Slack DM that nobody can audit.

How an engagement runs

Three fixes, in order

Sequence matters more than scope. Building automation on an undefined process just makes the wrong thing happen faster.

01

Agree on the definitions

What a lead is. What qualified means. When a stage advances and who is allowed to move it. One page, signed off by sales and marketing together, before anything is configured.

02

Rebuild on one source

Collapse the lifecycle, retire the fields nobody fills in, and put reporting on a single system of record so there is exactly one number to argue about.

03

Automate the handoff

Not the email. The handoff. Routing, ownership, alerts and SLA tracking, so the moment a deal changes hands is a system event rather than a favour.
